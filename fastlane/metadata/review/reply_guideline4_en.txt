Thank you for the detailed report. We reproduced the problem, found the cause,
and fixed it. A new build has been uploaded.

WHAT WAS WRONG
The app's screens are all designed for portrait, but the build incorrectly
declared support for landscape as well
(UISupportedInterfaceOrientations_iPhone listed Portrait, LandscapeLeft and
LandscapeRight). When the device was rotated, the content was laid out in a
portrait-shaped area on the left half of the screen while the right half stayed
black, and the primary button ("시작하기" / Start) moved off-screen entirely.
In that state a user could not get past the first screen.

This is almost certainly what you saw: the review was done on an iPad Air, and
an iPad is commonly held in landscape. We reproduced it exactly on an iPad Air
11-inch simulator and captured the broken frames.

WHAT WE CHANGED
The app is now locked to portrait. It is a one-handed app driven by an
on-screen number pad, so landscape serves no purpose here; the problem was that
the build claimed to support an orientation the app was never designed for, not
that a needed orientation was missing.

HOW WE VERIFIED IT
We added an automated UI regression test that rotates the device to
landscapeLeft and landscapeRight and then asserts (a) the primary button is
still hittable, not merely present, and (b) the app window keeps a portrait
aspect ratio. The test passes on iPhone 18 Pro and on iPad Air 11-inch, the
same device family used for the review. It would have failed on the build you
reviewed, where the window measured 2622x1206.

We also checked the smallest supported iPhone screen to confirm nothing is
clipped there.

SCOPE OF THE APP
The app is iPhone-only (TARGETED_DEVICE_FAMILY = 1) and portrait-only. On an
iPad it runs in iPhone compatibility mode, and with this fix it stays portrait
and is fully usable there, which we verified on the iPad Air simulator.

Please let us know if anything still looks wrong and we will address it right
away.
